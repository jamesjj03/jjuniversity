# Finishing Phase 2: geographic representation and source detail

## Projection decision

The previous Equal Earth decision was reopened after actual use. The new review
compared the same locked Natural Earth country geometry in six frames: the world,
Japan, India, China, Europe, and Benelux. Each frame compares Equal Earth, Robinson,
Miller cylindrical, and spherical Mercator. Review images are generated by
`scripts/compare-atlas-regional-projections.py` under
`output/atlas-finish/projections/`.

**Mercator is the implemented representation.** In the regional comparisons it
preserves the familiar Japan, India, China, and European shapes and keeps
north–south direction vertical. Miller improves directionality but still compresses
regional shapes. Equal Earth and Robinson retain the diagonal meridians that
contributed to the reported unfamiliar appearance. This is an authored product
choice, not a claim that Mercator is the most accurate way to compare country area.

Mercator enlarges high-latitude land substantially. Its initial inhabited-world
frame is narrower than Miller's. It does not show the mathematical poles. These
are explicit limitations, not new geographic facts. The canonical 242-entity
ontology and WGS84 source geometry are unchanged; Antarctica remains selectable
and below the initial frame, clipped only for rendering at ±85.05112878°.

Primary formula references:
- [PROJ Mercator documentation](https://proj.org/en/stable/operations/projections/merc.html)
- [PROJ Miller documentation](https://proj.org/en/stable/operations/projections/mill.html)

## Coordinate and camera contract

`lib/atlas-world/projection.ts` owns client/server point projection and authored
camera extents. The full fitted footprint is `[[289,14],[911,636]]` in the existing
1200 × 650 SVG coordinate space. The starting camera fits 60°S–80°N; that camera
choice does not remove geometry outside the frame. `projectAtlasWgs84` allows
authored labels, future points, and current selections to use canonical coordinates.

Country derivatives are explicitly named `geometry-mercator.v1.json` and
`geometry-mercator.v1.svg`. Physical derivatives and the population pyramid use
`projectionId: mercator`. Annotation focus and viewing bounds use generic
`projected` / `viewingBoundsProjected` names; highlight paths are regenerated
from their original WGS84 geometry by `scripts/reproject-atlas-notes.mjs`.

The raster target is `+proj=merc +R=6371007.180918475 +units=m +no_defs`.
Four dispersed landmarks test its registration against the exact SVG formula:
Nile delta, Himalayan foothills, Java, and Central Europe. All errors are below
0.008 viewBox units. Country source IDs, country facts, and all original 319 city
feature IDs remain intact.

## Actual source detail, not enlarged preview pixels

Population uses the existing locked **GHSL GHS-POP R2023A V1.0 2025 epoch,
one-kilometre equal-area source grid**. No additional demographic source was
introduced. Each display level is independently area-resampled from that original
source, then uses the same recorded log1p color/opacity transfer.

| Level | Equivalent global raster | Starts at zoom |
|---|---:|---:|
| Overview | 2,400 × 1,300 | Initial view |
| Regional | 19,200 × 10,400 | 4 |
| Country | 38,400 × 20,800 | 10 |
| Close | 76,800 × 41,600 | 20 |

The completed pyramid has 556 nonempty tiles: 46 regional (6,681,850 bytes),
133 country (22,541,976 bytes), and 377 close (77,665,666 bytes). These are global
download totals, not the initial page payload. The overview is 186,950 bytes.
Largest compressed detail tile: 1,771,848 bytes. The display scales are 4,022.33,
2,011.16, and 1,005.58 projected metres per pixel, respectively.

Only visible tiles of the active level are requested. Each tile is at most
1,200 × 1,300 pixels (6.24 MB decoded RGBA). The finest level is approximately
one kilometre per projected pixel at the equator; ground scale varies by latitude.
Further zoom does not add source observations. This remains modeled population,
not street-level population or a fresh census in every pixel. Successful tiles
replace overview pixels rather than doubling their opacity. Failed or pending
tiles preserve the overview fallback.

## More physical geography as the map gets closer

Three additional public-domain source files are pinned to Natural Earth repository
release **v5.1.2**, downloaded from its official project repository, and protected
by byte count and SHA-256 in `data/atlas/sources.lock.json`:

| Source | Original features | Source bytes | SHA-256 |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| 1:10m rivers/lake centerlines | 1,455 | 7,307,743 | `bb854a900ecbd3b408df46d5e16e3e0f974ba55993f9d8b5c26e855273c0905a` |
| 1:10m lakes | 1,355 | 5,043,554 | `2d036f53dedec578001c5c30c2959ee7d4eebc1306900fa4367c49929ec8f2d9` |
| 1:10m populated places | 7,342 | 19,359,003 | `9b8e3de09048ef00dfc70357dbb9fa324493f214b5e0ae4daf1aa79a8d10116b` |

The bounded selection contains **582 river features, 511 lake features, and 1,140
cities**. Original 1:50m rivers/lakes remain for world and regional views and for
stable annotation references. At zoom 6, separately identified 1:10m physical
features replace their display. More detailed systems enter at zoom 10 and 16,
using source `min_zoom` ranking. Empty source geometries and exact duplicates are
excluded rather than fabricating paths. Cities retain national capitals and
`SCALERANK <= 4`, with progressive visibility and stable Natural Earth IDs.

Physical paths live in two independently cached SVG resources: 171 coarse paths
in a 250,341-byte overview and 922 detailed paths in a 3,188,300-byte close-scale
resource. This avoids repeating path strings in both server HTML and React's
page data. The close resource is requested only when close geography is visible.
Canonical and derived vertices remain intact; there is no additional simplification
to achieve the payload reduction. Every sprite path is checked against its
source-derived geometry, and both sprite checksums are in the geography manifest.

[Natural Earth's source guidance](https://www.naturalearthdata.com/downloads/10m-physical-vectors/10m-rivers-lake-centerlines/)
describes these as cartographic river selections, not a complete hydrological
network. No discharge, navigability, actual water width, or seasonal extent is
inferred. The relief source remains the same generalized manually shaded raster;
it is not an elevation measurement or 3D terrain surface.

## Reproduce and verify

1. `npm run atlas:sources:fetch` (15 checksummed source locks).
2. `npm run atlas:world:build` (country facts remain unchanged; new projection).
3. `npm run atlas:geography:build` (requires the pinned Python 3.11 environment;
   regenerates imagery, vector derivatives, metadata, and annotation derivatives).
4. `npm run atlas:world:check`.
5. Run `tests/atlas/projection.spec.ts`, `label-geometry.spec.ts`,
   `raster-budget.spec.ts`, and `raster-detail.spec.ts` against the running
   application.

Projection, label geometry, and raster budget tests pass all 26 desktop/mobile
project combinations. Source verification confirms all 15 locked inputs.

## Browser payload and pan measurements

`scripts/atlas/measure-geography-performance.mjs` records the actual visible SVG
footprint (including the phone's cropped `slice` view), loaded tile IDs, requests,
and JavaScript long tasks during pans. Settled-code results and screenshots are
in `output/atlas-finish/geography-performance-final/`. These are headless Chromium
measurements on a desktop host, not an actual iPhone Safari or frame-rate claim.

External physical SVGs reduced initial Where People Live HTML from 13,676,283 raw
bytes / 2,269,871 gzip bytes to 7,528,985 raw / 636,169 gzip bytes. Fresh desktop
and phone contexts made 19 and 18 initial requests respectively: one coarse
physical SVG, no detailed physical SVG, and no density detail tiles. HTML still
has substantial serialized content; this result is a reduction, not zero cost.

All eight measured pan probes had no JavaScript tasks longer than 50 ms and no
page errors after the camera/cartography optimization. Loaded tiles exactly
matched the inverse-transformed visible viewport in every case:

| Viewport | Actual zoom | Density level | Visible tiles | Decoded RGBA ceiling |
|---|---:|---|---:|---:|
| Desktop 1440×900 | 6 | Regional | 12 | 74.88 MB |
| Desktop | 12 | Country | 12 | 74.88 MB |
| Desktop | 24 | Close | 8 | 49.92 MB |
| Desktop | 128 | Close | 2 | 12.48 MB |
| Phone 390×844 | 6.426 | Regional | 6 | 37.44 MB |
| Phone | 11.099 | Country | 4 | 24.96 MB |
| Phone | 21.794 | Close | 6 | 37.44 MB |
| Phone | 102.589 | Close | 1 | 6.24 MB |

Wheel targeting does not land at exactly the requested scale on the emulated
phone; the table reports observed scales rather than labeling that final probe
128. These ceilings count currently visible images, not all browser GPU/cache
memory. The global pyramid is never requested in full by a single view.

The 122 obsolete Equal Earth display derivatives were removed only after the
Mercator geography validation passed: the old country SVG/JSON, two overview
images, and 118 old density tiles. They remain recoverable from the pre-pass Git
baseline. Country facts, canonical source inputs, and unrelated JJU files were
not retired. The existing Equal Earth comparison formula remains available for
future representation comparisons; it is no longer mislabeled as the active view.
