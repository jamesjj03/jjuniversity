## Editorial recommendation

The AI-process paragraph is optional for Lulu print compliance, but it is the cleanest transparency choice because it matches the public About page without pretending the tools were absent or suggesting that an unedited model output is the author. If a copyright registration is later filed, disclose AI-generated material accurately in the registration application; the U.S. Copyright Office treats that as a separate registration requirement, not merely copyright-page wording.

The errors-and-corrections language is also optional as a matter of copyright notice, but is deliberately identified as an editorial disclaimer within the combined page. Copyright identifies ownership; it does not certify factual accuracy. The wording acknowledges JJ University's rapid publishing pace while giving readers useful correction information. Before each proof, correct known material errors where practical, then use a stable title/edition/printing-specific errata page for corrections discovered after the files are locked.

Do not claim that a volume is “fully verified,” “definitive,” or error-free while this note says corrections may remain. Books involving living-person allegations or medical, legal, or financial guidance need title-specific review rather than relying on this general language.

The notice itself should retain the three standard elements: ©, year of first publication, and copyright owner name. The U.S. Copyright Office says notice is optional for modern works but can provide legal benefits.
